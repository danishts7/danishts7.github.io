What to Sell on Amazon: Pick Your First Product by Budget

Every product with demand has competition. Start with your budget.

By Danish · Danishts, Karachi · 22 November 2026

Many new sellers ask the same question: what should I sell on Amazon? A better first question is: how much can I afford to risk? Your budget decides which products make sense for you. And remember, every product with demand has competition.

If you have a good budget

Consider products with strong demand and healthy profit margins. You can also look at products that need approval or have compliance requirements, if they suit you.

  • These products may have higher entry barriers.
  • But lower competition is not guaranteed.
  • Check all costs, risks and requirements before you invest.

If you have a small budget

  • Start small and simple: choose a lightweight, lower-risk product.
  • Keep the first order small. Improve quality, packaging or the listing as you learn.
  • Cap your PPC budget so ads cannot eat your capital.
  • Move to regulated products later, when you have more money and experience.
Research first. Take calculated risks. Protect your capital. No product is guaranteed. Do the research before you order stock.

A simple way to decide

  1. Write down your total budget for stock, shipping, fees, the listing and ads.
  2. Shortlist a few products with real demand, then check the competition and the margin.
  3. Count every cost, including Amazon fees. See the FBA fee changes coming in 2027.
  4. Plan the PPC budget before you pay for stock. Read why every launch needs a PPC plan.

For the full path from idea to launch, see the 7 steps from product research to launch and why some products fail in research.

Common questions

What should I sell on Amazon as a beginner?

There is no single best product. Choose by budget: with a small budget, pick a lightweight, lower-risk product and keep the first order small. With a good budget you can also consider products that need approval, after checking all costs and requirements.

Can I start Amazon FBA with a small budget?

Yes, but keep it simple: a lightweight product, a small first order and a capped PPC budget. A small budget means you need a tighter plan, not a bigger risk.

Do products with approval requirements have less competition?

Not always. They may have higher entry barriers, but lower competition is not guaranteed. Check the costs, risks and requirements before you invest.

How much should I spend on PPC for a new product?

Set a daily cap you can afford and plan it before you order stock. The right amount depends on your product and your margin, so work it out on paper first.

Is any Amazon product guaranteed to sell?

No. Demand, competition and execution all matter. That is why research comes first and why you should only risk what you can afford.

CCTV: DVR vs NVR vs IP Camera

What's best for your office? A simple guide to choosing the right CCTV system.

By Danish · Danishts, Karachi · 17 November 2026

When an office plans CCTV, the first question is usually: DVR or NVR? The answer depends on the type of camera. A DVR works with analog cameras. An NVR works with IP (network) cameras. Here is a simple comparison so you can choose without paying for something you do not need.

DVR with analog cameras

  • How it works: a coax cable runs from each camera to the DVR.
  • Pros: lower cost, and you can reuse old wiring.
  • Cons: lower picture quality and more cabling.

NVR with IP cameras

  • How it works: a network (LAN) cable runs from each camera, and PoE powers the camera over the same cable.
  • Pros: higher resolution, one cable per camera, and easy to expand.
  • Cons: higher cost, and the cameras need network security.
Both give you continuous recording, playback, motion search and mobile viewing. The difference is in cable, picture quality, cost and how easily you can grow the system.

What I would choose

  • Small office (1 hall, 2 cameras): a PoE NVR kit with 2 IP cameras. With a tight budget, a 4-channel DVR is a fair choice.
  • Bigger office: IP cameras with a PoE switch and an NVR, a separate VLAN for the cameras, a UPS and bigger storage.

Wired cameras are more reliable than WiFi cameras for recording, because recording needs a steady connection. This is the same idea as in LAN vs WiFi in the Office: devices that must always work belong on a cable.

Before you buy: 4 quick checks

  1. Count the cameras and decide where each one will look (door, hall, parking, cash counter).
  2. Check the existing wiring. Old coax cable can support a DVR, while IP cameras need network cable.
  3. Plan the storage. Decide how many days of recording you want to keep, then choose the disk size.
  4. Plan power and security. A UPS keeps recording during power cuts, and IP cameras must be secured. Read is your CCTV safe from hackers.

Common questions

What is the difference between DVR and NVR?

A DVR records video from analog cameras connected by coax cable. An NVR records video from IP cameras connected over a network cable, and PoE can power the cameras through the same cable.

Is NVR better than DVR?

For most new offices an NVR with IP cameras is the better long-term choice because of higher resolution, one cable per camera and easy expansion. A DVR can still make sense on a tight budget or when good coax wiring already exists.

Do IP cameras need a separate power cable?

Not when you use PoE. A PoE switch or a PoE NVR sends power and data through the same network cable.

Can I use WiFi cameras in an office?

You can, but wired cameras are more reliable for recording. WiFi can drop or get crowded, so use it only where running a cable is not possible.

Is a 4-channel DVR enough for a small office?

For a small space with a few cameras, yes. Count how many cameras you need now and leave room to add more later.

ACoS vs TACoS: What's the Difference?

Two numbers, two different questions.

By Danish · Danishts, Karachi · 18 November 2026

When you run Amazon ads, you will see two numbers again and again: ACoS and TACoS. They look similar, but they answer different questions. Knowing the difference helps you judge your ads properly instead of guessing.

ACoS: are my ads paying for themselves?

  • Formula: ad spend ÷ ad sales.
  • Looks at: your ads only.
  • Asks: are my ads paying for themselves?

TACoS: how much of my total sales goes to ads?

  • Formula: ad spend ÷ total sales.
  • Looks at: ads plus organic sales.
  • Asks: how much of my total sales goes to ads?

Example (made-up numbers)

Ad spend $100, ad sales $400 and total sales $1,000.

  • ACoS = 100 ÷ 400 = 25%
  • TACoS = 100 ÷ 1,000 = 10%

These numbers are only an example. Real numbers are different for every product.

Check both. Compare ACoS with your margin. If your ACoS is higher than the margin you have before ad cost, each ad sale can lose money, so work out your margin first.

How to use them together

ACoS tells you how well your ads convert spend into ad sales. TACoS tells you how much your total sales depend on ads. When organic sales grow, TACoS usually goes down even if ad spend stays the same. Common questions

What is the difference between ACoS and TACoS?

ACoS is ad spend divided by ad sales, so it looks at your ads only. TACoS is ad spend divided by total sales (ads plus organic), so it shows how much of your total sales goes to ads.

How do I calculate TACoS?

Divide your ad spend by your total sales. For example, $100 of ad spend and $1,000 of total sales gives a TACoS of 10%.

What is a good ACoS?

It depends on your profit margin. Compare ACoS with the margin you have before ad cost, because an ACoS above that margin means each ad sale can lose money.

Should I look at ACoS or TACoS?

Both. ACoS shows how your ads perform, and TACoS shows how much your whole business depends on ads.

Can TACoS be lower than ACoS?

Yes, and it is common when you also have organic sales, because total sales are larger than ad sales.
